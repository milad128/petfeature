# PM Learning Roadmap — Beginner to CPO

> A structured, opinionated learning path for product managers at every career stage.
> Organised by **competency**, not by topic — built on Marty Cagan's *Empowered* framework.
> Last validated: July 2026.

---

## How to read this document

The roadmap has two layers.

**The competency depth matrix** says *how good you need to be* at each of 15 competencies, at each of 6 levels. It is the assessment rubric — your gap on any competency is `required depth at your target level − your current depth`.

**The six level roadmaps** say *what to study* to close those gaps. Each level sorts its resources into four categories that follow the arc of holding the job:

| Category | Question it answers | When | Resources |
|----------|--------------------|------|-----------|
| **Entry** | What do I need to *get* this job? | Before the title | 3–5 total |
| **Core** | What am I personally accountable for, daily? | Months 1–6 | 2 required + 1 optional each |
| **Supporting** | What do I contribute to but not own? | Months 6–18 | 1 required + 1 optional each |
| **Bridge** | What do I need to *ask for the promotion*? | Last ~6 months | 2 total |

**Core and Supporting split on accountability, not difficulty.** Core is where your name is on it. Supporting is where you feed someone else's decision — you need enough to participate credibly, not to lead.

### How resources are assigned

- **Core** — competencies reaching the level's *highest* required depth. Two required resources, one optional.
- **Supporting** — competencies that rise, but below the level's top depth. One required, one optional.
- **Maintain** — required depth is unchanged from the previous level. **No new study.** Listed so you know it still counts.
- **Acquired free** — depth 1. No resource assigned. You get awareness by sitting in the room; reading a strategy classic at depth 1 doesn't bank the learning, it burns the book.
- **⇈** marks a two-step jump (e.g. 1→3). Expect it to take roughly twice as long as a single step.

**Bridge material may preview the next level's Core.** That is deliberate — the bridge is a first pass under low stakes, the core is mastery under real ones.

### Three numbers, not one

Every competency carries three numbers. They measure different things and must not be added together.

- **Reading** — hours to get through the resources. *The Mom Test* is 3 hours.
- **Sprint** — the weeks where this competency is your **single active improvement project**. You read the resources, do the practice exercise, and get feedback on the output. **One at a time, strictly sequential.** This is a real budget you can sum.
- **Maturation** — elapsed months until you are actually at the required depth. It begins when the sprint begins and continues through ordinary work long after the sprint ends. **These overlap freely and are never summed.**

Product Discovery at APM is an **8-week sprint** and a **6-month maturation**. You spend two months deliberately working on interviewing; it then keeps ripening for four more months while you do your job, because the reps come from the job rather than from a study block.

The distinction matters because summing maturation windows produces a frightening, meaningless number. Three competencies each maturing over months 1–6 is six months of calendar time, not eighteen months of work.

**Sprint length** scales with target depth and reading volume — roughly 2–3 weeks at depth 2, 6 weeks at depth 3, 6–8 weeks at depth 4, 8 weeks at depth 5, plus 2 weeks for a multi-step jump.

**Maturation** starts from target depth:

| Target depth | Base maturation |
|---|---|
| 2 — Guided | 2 months |
| 3 — Independent | 4 months |
| 4 — Leads | 6 months |
| 5 — Defines | 9 months |

Then two modifiers, each **+50%**:

- **⇈ multi-step jump** — you are crossing two or more depths at once.
- **Habit competency** — depth comes from repeated reps with real people, not from pages. Discovery, Delivery, Communication, Experimentation, Stakeholder Influence, People Leadership, and Coaching are habit competencies. Technical Literacy, Data, Business, Vision, Strategy, Prioritization, and Org Design are knowledge competencies and mature faster.

**Every level's deliberate study fits comfortably inside its tenure** — between 4.5 and 13.5 months of sprints everywhere. The rest of the tenure is maturation, which is not idle time and cannot be accelerated by working harder. That gap is the honest answer to why levels take years when the reading takes weeks.

## Level summary

| # | Level | Specification | Required | Reading | Sprints | Tenure |
|---|-------|--------------|----------|---------|---------|--------|
| 1 | **Beginner (APM)** | Learns fundamentals, contributes to execution under guidance, develops core analytical, technical, and communication skills. | 18 | ~57h | 31 wk (~7 mo) | 12–24 months |
| 2 | **Product Manager** | Independently owns a product or feature, discovers customer problems, prioritizes solutions, and delivers measurable business outcomes. | 18 | ~81h | 58 wk (~13.5 mo) | 24–36 months |
| 3 | **Senior Product Manager** | Solves ambiguous product problems, leads strategic initiatives, mentors other PMs, and influences direction across multiple teams. | 16 | ~86h | 54 wk (~12.5 mo) | 24–36 months |
| 4 | **Product Lead** | Leads a small group of PMs, sets direction for a significant product area, bridges IC excellence and people leadership. | 8 | ~49h | 19 wk (~4.5 mo) | 12–24 months |
| 5 | **Director of Product** | Builds and leads product teams, defines strategy and operating processes, aligns investment with business objectives. | 15 | ~83h | 52 wk (~12 mo) | 36–60 months |
| 6 | **CPO** | Owns the company's product vision and portfolio, shapes long-term strategy, and aligns executive leadership around market opportunities. | 11 | ~65h | 44 wk (~10 mo) | Open-ended |

> **Tenure** = how long to actually work at that level before levelling up. **Sprints** = total weeks of deliberate, one-at-a-time study. The gap between the two columns is maturation — real, unavoidable, and impossible to accelerate.

**Full path: 86 required resources · ~421 hours of reading · 258 sprint-weeks (~60 months) · 10–17 years of career time**

### Reading hours lie; the tenure gap doesn't

The useful comparison is not which level has the most reading — it is how much of each tenure is *not* study:

| Level | Sprints | Tenure (mid) | Maturation-only |
|---|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| L1 APM | ~7 mo | 18 mo | **61%** |
| L2 PM | ~13.5 mo | 30 mo | **55%** |
| L3 Senior PM | ~12.5 mo | 30 mo | **58%** |
| **L4 Product Lead** | **~4.5 mo** | **18 mo** | **75%** |
| L5 Director | ~12 mo | 48 mo | **75%** |
| L6 CPO | ~10 mo | — | — |

**L4 is the lightest level to study and the one people most often fail.** Three quarters of it is maturation on habit competencies whose feedback loops you do not control — you cannot tell whether your coaching worked until month eight. A Senior PM who was promoted by out-working everyone arrives here and finds the lever disconnected. That mismatch between apparent effort and real difficulty is the single most useful thing these numbers expose.

```
Beginner (APM) → PM → Senior PM → Product Lead → Director → CPO
```

Note that Level 4 has the *fewest* resources and the *shortest* time in role. It is a bridge, not a knowledge level — the work is unlearning, not accumulating.

## Competency depth matrix

The six levels each list five *core outcomes* (Cagan / *Empowered*), but those are altitude-renamed versions of a smaller set of underlying competencies — "Product Strategy" at PM, "Product Area Strategy" at Lead, and "Portfolio Strategy" at Director are one competency at three depths. The matrix below consolidates all 30 core outcomes into 15 competencies and states **how deep each one must go at each level**.

### Depth scale

| | Level | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Aware | Knows the vocabulary, can follow the conversation, can't produce |
| **2** | Guided | Can do it with support or a template |
| **3** | Independent | Does it unaided, well, at their own scope |
| **4** | Leads | Sets the approach for others, coaches it, raises the bar |
| **5** | Defines | Sets how the whole organization does it |
| **–** | — | Not required at this level |

The scale measures **required mastery, not hands-on frequency**. A CPO scores 5 on discovery because she defines the organization's discovery capability — not because she runs the interviews herself.

### Reading the matrix

**Four competencies decline or flatten — that is the most useful signal here.**

- **Delivery & Execution peaks at L2–L3 and falls to 2.** This is the most common failure mode in the move to Lead and Director: the leader who keeps running the sprint. You are supposed to get *less* hands-on here.
- **Technical Literacy is flat at 3 across all six levels.** It is a threshold, not a ladder — reach credible-conversations-with-engineers and stop.
- **Market & Competitive Insight dips at L4** (4 → 3 → 4). Not an error. Product Lead is the management bridge — its entire curriculum is people skills. Strategic breadth pauses while leadership catches up.
- **Experimentation plateaus at 4.** Few organizations need one person to define experimentation company-wide.

**Three competencies are step functions, not ramps.** People Leadership (– → 3 at L4), Coaching (1 → 3 at L3), and Org Design & Culture (1 → 4 at L5) each jump two or more points at a single boundary. Those jumps *are* the L3→L4 and L4→L5 promotions. Everything else drifts upward gradually; these are the actual gates.

**The L1→L2 gate is different in kind.** No single row jumps alone. Instead eight rows move together — Data & Goal-setting 1→4, Prioritization 2→4, Product Strategy 1→3, Experimentation 1→3, Market 1→3, Discovery 3→4, Vision 1→2, Business 1→2. APM→PM is not one new skill; it is the whole craft deepening from "can do" to "leads." That is why it is the most common plateau: there is no single thing to study your way past.

**Data & Goal-setting 1→4 is the largest single jump in the matrix** — three depths at one boundary, larger than People Leadership at L4. An APM is not expected to own a metric; a PM is accountable for one. There is no gentle version of that transition, which is why the L1 Bridge carries a metrics resource and L2 treats Data as its heaviest core competency.

**An APM's job is delivery.** L1 requires depth 3 on only four competencies — delivery, research, writing, and technical literacy. Six of the fifteen sit at depth 1 and get no study at all. Metrics and commercial thinking arrive at L2, not L1.

**Levels 1–3 are IC competencies; 4–6 are leadership competencies.** Stopping at Senior PM is a legitimate destination, not a failure to climb.

### A note on Product Discovery

**Product Discovery** consolidates two of Cagan's core outcomes — "Customer Knowledge" at APM and "Continuous Discovery" at PM. At depth 3 it means you can run an interview and synthesize what you heard. At depth 4 it is a weekly habit with opportunity solution trees. At depth 5 it is an organizational capability you instill rather than a practice you run.

But discovery in the full sense — de-risking **value, usability, feasibility, and viability** before you build — deliberately spans four rows of this matrix, because its parts mature at different rates and belong to different halves of the job:

| Part of discovery | Competency |
|---|---|
| Talking to users, synthesizing, finding the real problem | **Product Discovery** |
| Assumption testing, prototyping before building | **Experimentation & Validation** |
| Opportunity solution trees, choosing which problem to pursue | **Prioritization & Tradeoffs** |
| Understanding the job the product is hired to do | **Market & Competitive Insight** |

If you are assessing yourself on "how good am I at discovery?", answer it four times.

## Core — your daily job

### 1 · Delivery & Execution → 3
**Sprint: 6 weeks** · Matures over 6 months · Reading: 6h · *Habit competency*

The spine of the role. This is what you do every day, from week one. Six months to mature because you need to live through several full cycles — planning, slippage, retro, and the fix that follows — before the process stops being theatre.

| Resource | Type | Time | Diff | Persian | |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| *Scrum* — Jeff Sutherland | Book | 4h | ⭐ | 🇮🇷 | required |
| Atlassian Agile Coach | Guide (free) | 2h | ⭐ | 🔴 | required |
| *Shape Up* — Ryan Singer | Book (free online) | 5h | ⭐⭐ | 🔴 | optional |

> **Why *Scrum* before *Shape Up*?** Scrum is what most teams run; Shape Up is the alternative you'll meet later. Know the default first.

**Practice:** Map your team's idea→shipped flow as a diagram. Mark where it stalls.

### 2 · Product Discovery → 3
**Sprint: 8 weeks** · Matures over 6 months · Reading: 5h · *Habit competency*

The longest sprint at this level, and it comes second because it gates the quality of every spec you will write afterward. You can't spec what you don't understand.

*The Mom Test* takes 3 hours; the competency takes 6 months to mature. **Interview targets: 5 during the sprint** (the practice exercise), **~20 by the end of maturation**, **10 is the promotion floor.** Reps cannot be compressed — you need the gap between them to synthesize, and you need enough of them to hear yourself ask a leading question, notice it, and stop.

| Resource | Type | Time | Diff | Persian | |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| *The Mom Test* — Rob Fitzpatrick | Book | 3h | ⭐ | 🇮🇷 | required |
| Nielsen Norman Group — interview articles + videos | Articles (free) | 2h | ⭐ | 🔴 | required |
| *Interviewing Users* — Steve Portigal | Book | 6h | ⭐⭐ | 🔴 | optional |

> **Why *The Mom Test* first?** It's the fastest path from zero to not-wasting-your-interviews. Read it in one sitting before your first user call.

**Practice:** Conduct 5 interviews using only Mom Test question style — no solution pitching, no leading questions. Write up the top 3 patterns you heard across all five.

### 3 · Communication & Writing → 3
**Sprint: 6 weeks** · Matures over 6 months · Reading: 4.5h · *Habit competency*

The artifact your delivery work produces. A spec nobody reads doesn't exist. Reading takes an afternoon; writing specs that engineers stop asking questions about takes a dozen specs and the feedback from each. Sequenced after research, so the specs you practise on are informed by someone real.

| Resource | Type | Time | Diff | Persian | |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| *Writing for Busy Readers* — Rogers & Levy | Book | 4h | ⭐ | 🔴 | required |
| PRD templates — Lenny Rachitsky | Template (free) | 30m | ⭐ | 🔴 | required |
| GitLab Product Handbook — spec writing | Guide (free) | 1h | ⭐ | 🔴 | optional |

> **Why *Writing for Busy Readers* over a PM-specific writing guide?** PM writing fails because people don't read it. This book solves the reading problem, not the writing problem.

**Practice:** Write a full PRD for a feature you use every day. Include problem, user story, acceptance criteria, edge cases, and what you're explicitly *not* building.

### 4 · Technical Literacy → 3
**Sprint: 6 weeks** · Matures over 4 months · Reading: 11h · *Knowledge competency*

Threshold skill — reach 3 and stop. It is the only competency that never rises again across all six levels. It also carries the **most reading hours in Core and the shortest maturation**, because it is the only knowledge competency here. That inversion is the clearest illustration of why reading and maturation are tracked separately.

| Resource | Type | Time | Diff | Persian | |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Khan Academy: Computer Science | Course (free) | 5h | ⭐ | 🔴 | required |
| AI for Everyone — Andrew Ng | Course (free to audit) | 6h | ⭐ | 🔴 | required |
| CS50 — Harvard, first weeks only | Course (free) | 6h | ⭐⭐ | 🔴 | optional |

> **Why not a full programming course?** You're not becoming an engineer. Target: understand what's hard, what's easy, and what's possible. Stop there.

**Practice:** Have an engineer explain one feature technically. Explain it back to a non-technical person. Both directions working = you're at 3.

## Sequencing — 31 sprint-weeks across an 18-month tenure

| Weeks | Sprint — one at a time | Maturing in background |
|---|---|---|
| **1–6** | Delivery & Execution | — |
| **7–14** | Product Discovery | Delivery |
| **15–20** | Communication & Writing | Delivery, Research |
| **21–26** | Technical Literacy | Research, Writing |
| **27–29** | Stakeholder & Exec Influence | Writing, Technical Literacy |
| **30–31** | Prioritization & Tradeoffs | Technical Literacy, Stakeholder |
| **Months 8–15** | *No sprints* | Everything |
| **Months 15–18** | Bridge — reading only | — |

**Delivery goes first, research second.** You are in a sprint from week one whether you are ready or not; research follows immediately with the longest window at this level, because it gates the quality of every spec you write afterward.

**Months 8–15 have no sprints at all, and that is the point.** All deliberate study is done by month 8. The remaining half of your tenure is where the competencies actually ripen through ordinary work — nothing left to read, everything still happening. If you feel idle here, you have misread what the level is for.

**If your tenure runs 12 months**, compress the maturation gap rather than the sprints — but expect to arrive at PM with delivery and research still green. **If it runs 24**, the slack goes into more interview reps, not more books.

*Reading only — no sprint. Months 15–18.*

Eight competencies jump at this boundary: **Data 1→4 ⇈** (the largest jump in the matrix), Prioritization 2→4 ⇈, Strategy 1→3 ⇈, Experimentation 1→3 ⇈, Market 1→3 ⇈, Discovery 3→4, Vision 1→2, Business 1→2. These three resources cover the cheapest of them and blunt the steepest.

### Ready for PM when:
- Shipped at least one feature end-to-end with minimal hand-holding
- Can explain *why* a decision was made, not just what was built
- Conducted 10+ user interviews and synthesized findings into clear insights
- Can read the team's dashboard and ask a sensible question about it
- Writes specs engineers can build from without follow-up clarification

## Core — your daily job

### 1 · Product Discovery → 4
**Sprint: 8 weeks** · Matures over 9 months · Reading: 7h+ · *Habit competency*

Discovery stops being a project phase and becomes a weekly habit. This is the competency that separates good PMs from great ones. Nine months to mature is roughly how long it takes for weekly interviews to survive a quarter where everything is on fire — which is the real test of whether it's a habit or a phase.

| Resource | Type | Time | Diff | Persian | |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| *Continuous Discovery Habits* — Teresa Torres | Book | 7h | ⭐⭐ | 🔴 | required |
| producttalk.org — Teresa Torres | Blog (free) | ongoing | ⭐⭐ | 🔴 | required |
| *The Right It* — Alberto Savoia | Book | 5h | ⭐⭐ | 🔴 | optional |

> **Why Torres over Cagan here?** Cagan tells you *that* you should do discovery. Torres tells you *how* to do it weekly, with a real team.

**Practice:** For 2 consecutive weeks, run at least 2 interviews per week without pitching a solution. Map findings on an opportunity solution tree. Identify the top 3 unmet needs.

### 2 · Prioritization & Tradeoffs → 4 ⇈
**Sprint: 8 weeks** · Matures over 9 months · Reading: 11h · *Knowledge competency, two-step jump*

Double jump. You now own the backlog and must defend every "no." The maturation months go into defending real decisions to real stakeholders who wanted a different answer.

| Resource | Type | Time | Diff | Persian | |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| *The Principles of Product Development Flow* — Reinertsen | Book | 10h | ⭐⭐⭐ | 🔴 | required |
| GIST framework — Itamar Gilad | Articles (free) | 1h | ⭐⭐ | 🔴 | required |
| *Inspired* — Cagan, roadmap chapters | Book | 2h | ⭐⭐ | 🔴 | optional |

> **Why not just use RICE?** RICE is a tool, not a framework. Reinertsen teaches the economics underneath — read him so RICE makes sense.

**Practice:** Score your backlog with RICE. Re-score using cost of delay. Present the difference and what it changes.

### 3 · Data, Metrics & Goal-setting → 4 ⇊
**Sprint: 10 weeks** · Matures over 9 months · Reading: 16h · *Knowledge competency, three-step jump*

**The largest jump in the entire matrix — 1 → 4 in one boundary.** An APM is never asked to own a metric; a PM is accountable for one. You arrive here owing three depths, which is why this carries the longest sprint and the most reading of any competency at this level.

Nine months to mature because you need to set a metric, ship against it, and be wrong at least once. The being-wrong is the part that teaches.

## Core — your daily job

### 1 · Product Strategy → 4
**Sprint: 8 weeks** · Matures over 6 months · Reading: 14h · *Knowledge competency*

Tell the difference between a real strategy and a list of goals — then build real ones.

| *Thinking in Systems* — Donella Meadows | Book | 6h | ⭐⭐ | 🔴 | optional |

> **Why Rumelt first?** Most frameworks teach you to fill in boxes. Rumelt teaches you what strategy *is*. After Rumelt you'll recognize bad strategy everywhere — including your own.

**Practice:** Write a 1-page strategy for your area using Rumelt's kernel — diagnosis, guiding policy, coherent actions. Share it with a senior leader and get brutal feedback.

### 2 · Product Vision → 4 ⇈
**Sprint: 8 weeks** · Matures over 9 months · Reading: 2.25h · *Knowledge competency, two-step jump*

Double jump. You now craft the vision rather than repeat it. The starkest reading-to-focus ratio in the entire roadmap: barely two hours of material, nine months to get good. There is nothing to read because vision isn't knowledge — it's the accumulated judgment of watching your own drafts fail to move anyone.

**Practice:** Write a 2–3 sentence vision for your area, no jargon, that a new engineer could read and immediately know what you're building and why it matters. Read it to 3 people and ask what they think you're building.

### 3 · Communication & Writing → 4
**Sprint: 8 weeks** · Matures over 9 months · Reading: 12h · *Habit competency*

### 6 · Experimentation & Validation → 4
**Sprint: 6 weeks** · Matures over 9 months · Reading: 12h+ · *Habit competency*

## Core — your daily job

### 1 · People Leadership → 3 ⇈
**Sprint: 8 weeks** · Matures over 9 months · Reading: 19h · *Habit competency, two-step jump*

Double jump from nothing. Your new job is making others successful — not being the smartest PM in the room. This single competency absorbs what used to be four separate topics: managing, 1:1s, delegation, and performance.

Nine months is the floor, and it is set by feedback cycles you don't control: you need to give hard feedback and watch what happens over the following weeks, several times, with different people. You cannot batch that.

| Resource | Type | Time | Diff | Persian | |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| *The Making of a Manager* — Julie Zhuo | Book | 6h | ⭐ | 🔴 | required |

**Practice:** Write one objective and three key results for the next quarter. Make each KR a leading indicator you can influence, not a lagging one you can only observe. Check whether your team can connect daily work to each KR.

### 4 · Coaching, Hiring & Talent → 5
**Sprint: 8 weeks** · Matures over 14 months · Reading: 14h · *Habit competency*

You can't build a great org without great people, and you can't keep them without developing them.

The longest single focus window in the roadmap. Hiring feedback loops are brutally slow — you cannot judge a hire for six months, and you cannot judge your *hiring process* until several hires have played out. Fourteen months buys you one honest look at whether your scorecard predicts anything.

| Resource | Type | Time | Diff | Persian | |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| *Who* — Geoff Smart & Randy Street | Book | 5h | ⭐⭐ | 🔴 | required |
| *Work Rules!* — Laszlo Bock | Book | 9h | ⭐⭐ | 🔴 | required |
| *First, Break All the Rules* — Buckingham & Coffman | Book | 7h | ⭐⭐ | 🔴 | optional |

> **Why *Who* over a PM-specific hiring guide?** Smart & Street's A-method is the most rigorous hiring process available. Apply it to PM hiring and your false-positive rate drops sharply.

**Practice:** Write a scorecard for your next hire — 5–7 outcomes for their first year, the competencies each requires, and the interview questions that reveal them. Separately, run a talent calibration: rate each PM on a 3×3 performance/potential grid with one development action each.

## Core — your daily job

### 1 · Market & Competitive Insight → 5
**Sprint: 8 weeks** · Matures over 9 months · Reading: 13h · *Knowledge competency*

Identify the structural forces that determine whether you win over the long run.

| Resource | Type | Time | Diff | Persian | |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| *7 Powers* — Hamilton Helmer | Book | 5h | ⭐⭐⭐ | 🔴 | required |
| *The Innovator's Dilemma* — Clayton Christensen | Book | 8h | ⭐⭐⭐ | 🇮🇷 | required |
| *The Innovator's Solution* — Christensen | Book | 8h | ⭐⭐⭐ | 🔴 | optional |

> **Why *7 Powers* over Porter?** Porter gives you the vocabulary. Helmer gives you the power analysis — which of the 7 powers do you actually have, and how do you build more? The most practically useful competitive framework for a CPO.

> **Why both Christensen books?** *Dilemma* diagnoses the failure mode; *Solution* gives the playbook for avoiding it. Read them back to back.

**Practice:** Map your company against Helmer's 7 powers — which do you have, which is strongest, which could you build in 3 years? Then write a 1-page "disruptor brief": who would build the simpler, cheaper alternative to your core product, who would buy it, and what you should do now.

**Sprint: 8 weeks** · Matures over 9 months · Reading: 11h · *Knowledge competency*

A long-horizon vision that unifies every product team. Nine months because a company vision has to survive contact with the whole organisation — you write it, watch which parts people repeat back and which they quietly drop, and rewrite.

**Practice:** Write a 3-year product thesis — what the market will look like, where you need to be positioned, the 3 bets to get there. Include explicit assumptions: what has to be true, and what you'd do if each turned out false.

### 3 · Org Design & Culture → 5
**Sprint: 8 weeks** · Matures over 9 months · Reading: 14h · *Knowledge competency*

Build a product org that scales from 10 to 200+ without losing speed, and a culture where great product thinking is the norm. Reorgs are annual events at best — this window is one full cycle of designing a structure, living inside it, and seeing what it actually produced.

## Idea — Seniority assessment → personalized learning path

> **Status:** Raw idea — not scoped, not versioned. Captured July 2026.
> **Depends on:** v12 User Auth (logged-in users only) + this roadmap as the content backbone.
> **Related:** [Resume Feedback Agent](./resume-feedback-agent.md) — sibling idea that reuses the same résumé upload to critique the CV itself.

### One-liner

A logged-in user is assessed against the competency depth matrix, placed at one of the 6 levels, and given a **personalized learning path** — the right competencies and resources for where she is now, ordered by her largest gaps. The path is pinned to her **dashboard**, where she follows it resource by resource and tracks progress.

### Why the matrix makes this work

The matrix, not the résumé, is the assessment instrument. A user rates herself 1–5 on each of 15 competencies; the system compares her ratings against the target level's column. `Required depth − self-rated depth` produces a ranked gap list, and the largest gaps determine which competencies surface first in her path. That is a deterministic, explainable mechanism — no model required.

### Two entry points, one generator

- **Self-assessment (primary):** 15 competency sliders plus a few context questions — years in role, scope owned, team size, and *"are you currently working as a PM?"* (that last one decides whether the Acquired-free competencies need resources after all). No PII, no file upload, no API cost.
- **Résumé upload (optional, more accurate):** PDF/DOCX → text extraction → AI assessment against the same matrix. Catches what self-assessment misses — people systematically misjudge their own strategy and influence depth.

Both produce the same output: an assessed level, a ranked gap list, and an ordered path. The assessed level is always **a suggestion the user can override**.

### How it connects to existing/planned features

- **Content backbone:** this document *is* the source catalogue of levels, competencies, and resources.
- **Bookshelf (v15):** the mark-as-read mechanic already exists — a learning path is a curated, ordered bookshelf with a level attached. Books already marked "خواندم" should pre-mark as path progress.
- **User Dashboard (v14):** the path lives here as a "مسیر یادگیری من" section.
- **Resume Feedback Agent:** shares the résumé upload + parse primitive — build once, power both.

### Progress tracking

- Each path = an ordered list of competencies and resources with per-item status (نخوانده / در حال خواندن / خواندم).
- A progress indicator: "۳ از ۱۲ منبع مسیر — ۲۵٪".
- Completing a level's Bridge suggests re-assessment for the next level.

### Open questions (for when this gets scoped)

- **Path shape:** the whole level, or Core-only first to avoid overwhelming? (Core alone is 8 resources at L1 — a much friendlier starting commitment.)
- **Re-assessment:** can the user re-take the assessment later to re-level as she grows? (Should be yes; keep old paths inactive rather than deleting.)
- **Persian filtering:** offer a "فقط منابع فارسی" toggle — noting it produces an empty result at L4.
- **Data model:** `LearningPath` (user_id, assessed_level, source, rationale, created_at, is_active) + `LearningPathItem` (path_id, resource ref, order, status).
- **Privacy / model / cost / file handling:** same concerns as the [Resume Feedback Agent](./resume-feedback-agent.md#open-questions-for-when-this-gets-scoped) — lean toward not retaining the file.
- **Resource-to-catalogue linking:** resources here need to map to actual `Book` records in the library where they exist; most are English titles not yet in the library.

### Not in scope

- Certificates or formal credentials on completion
- Social/leaderboard comparison of progress between users
- Auto-generating new learning content — the path only sequences existing resources
- Job matching based on assessed level

*Raw idea — needs a PRD before any build. Blocked on v12 User Auth; strongly synergistic with v14 (Dashboard) and v15 (Bookshelf).*
